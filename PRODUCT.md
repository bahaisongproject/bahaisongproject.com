# Product

## Register

product

## Users

People who want to find, learn, listen to, play, sing, share, or improve Bahai songs for gatherings, devotionals, children's classes, Feasts, study circles, and community life. They may arrive with a song already in mind, browse for something that fits a moment, or need a practical way to use lyrics, chords, recordings, and song sheets on a phone or laptop.

## Product Purpose

Bahai Song Project is a public songbook and song-resource product. It helps people discover songs, understand whether a song fits their context, listen to available renditions, access lyrics and chords through song sheets or the newer Songbook, and contribute improvements so the collection stays useful. Success means a visitor can get from intention to usable song with minimal friction and can trust the resource enough to return, share it, and use it in community settings.

## Brand Personality

Warm, devotional, practical. The product should feel welcoming and reverent without becoming precious, polished without becoming promotional, and simple enough that the music remains the center of attention.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic SaaS landing-page treatment, flashy worship-media aesthetics, dry archive or library-only presentation, and decorative music-site gloss that gets in the way of song use. The interface should not feel transactional, overproduced, trend-driven, or visually busy when the user is trying to find and use a song.

## Design Principles

1. Put the song within reach: search, browse, metadata, listening, song sheets, and Songbook links should all help the user move quickly toward singing or playing.
2. Serve the gathering: prioritize mobile readability, clear actions, and low-friction sharing for people using the site in real community moments.
3. Let warmth come through restraint: the visual system should feel human and devotional while keeping utility, clarity, and legibility first.
4. Make contribution feel natural: improving or adding songs should feel like caring for a shared resource, not entering an administrative process.
5. Preserve trust: metadata, external links, PDFs, embeds, and contribution paths should feel consistent, reliable, and easy to understand.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use WCAG AA as the baseline. Prioritize readable contrast, keyboard-friendly search and navigation, clear focus states, accessible form controls, reduced-motion support, and color-blind-safe use of tags, states, and accents. The site should work well on phones and laptops, including for people using it in live gatherings where attention, lighting, and time may be limited.
